Graduate school is many things at once — a sequence of proofs, a long reading list, a community of people thinking hard about the same problems. Here are a few sketches from my day-to-day.

---

### Coursework

My training at Yale spans graduate econometrics (with Edward Vytlacil, Econ 5551), empirical asset pricing, and accounting theory — the slow sequence that turns intuitions into proofs. Much of my work involves IV/TSLS, measurement error, asymptotic theory, propensity scores, and linear projection, often worked through from first principles in my own LaTeX notes.

### New Haven

Walks between Evans Hall and the SOM. The occasional detour through Bass Library when a proof won't cooperate. Quiet afternoons in the Beinecke. New Haven is a small city that grows on you — slowly, then all at once.

### Seminars & Talks

I've presented on Dube & Zhu (2021), discussed fair-value papers by Cianciaruso et al., and sat in on finance and accounting workshops most Fridays. Seminars are where ideas get stress-tested — and where I learn the most about how to think like a researcher.
