# An analogy of sorts

In my work I have found my drafts and ideas have developed from internal thoughts through to documents supporting varying angles of the process however in the mass of notes and documents I can never seem to find the key documents when I need them. One way to make notes searchable and re-discoverable, quick and easy to find? I have realised this concept can fit with the develepment of static websites through RMarkdown. 

Most sites and blogs you create will never be seen by the people you want to see it. Thats ok as its a great way to practise writing and documenting work so that others (and yourself) can come back to it in the future and understand how it all works.

## Background

Keeping up with new literature and news along side developing and documenting research for publication is a challenging path for a range of reasons. Publishing research through a range of platforms have become a common practise in the extension of research outcomes. These are my notes on the process and possible advantage of seeing blogs and websites as a platform for drafts and other less structured research applications.

### Software or programming language

There are many options to create and publish a website, a few of the popular ones in the current enviroment are:

```
- Wordpress
- Jekyll
- Hugo
- Java
- etc
```

### Database

With database driven websites like Wordpress there are a collection of other frameworks needed to host a live website others can interact with over the web. An alternative options is to build a static website with a site generator such as Jekyll or Rmarkdown.

### Platform

This is a hard one as there are many options that are limited by budget and skill set. Here I will only describe the platform setup I use... `github` and wordpress.

## github vs. wordpress
