The PhD is a long road, and I've found it's easier when walked together. Below are a few notes I've assembled for current and incoming students — things I've learned the hard way, or wish I'd known sooner.

---

### On Coursework

**Derive, don't memorize.** For econometrics especially, work through every step of every proof. Shortcuts feel fast but collapse under pressure. I keep a LaTeX notebook for each course — FWL, measurement error, asymptotics — all re-derived in my own notation.

**Read problem sets like papers.** Treat each problem as a small research question. What's the DGP? What's identified? What assumption is doing the work? The habit transfers directly to reading literature.

**Build a personal reference.** I keep one LaTeX document per topic that I can come back to years later. Future-you will be grateful.

---

### On Idea Generation

**Read across, not just down.** The best ideas often live at the intersection of fields. War bonds × short selling × earnings management isn't in any one literature — it emerges when you let threads cross.

**Keep an "open questions" log.** Every paper has an unanswered question in the conclusion or tucked into a footnote. Collect them. A lot of dissertation chapters start as someone else's footnote.

**Test the question before the answer.** Before running regressions, ask: if I had perfect data, would the answer be interesting either way? If not, refine the question first.

---

### On Data & Coding

**Know your identifiers.** GVKEY, CIK, PERMNO, RCID — crosswalks are where empirical projects live or die. Build a clean linking table early and treat it as sacred.

**fixest + data.table for panels.** In R, this combination is wonderfully fast for high-dimensional fixed effects. Worth the learning curve.

**Reproducibility is a gift to future-you.** Every project: a master script, seeds for randomness, a README with WRDS access notes. Replication isn't optional — it's care.
